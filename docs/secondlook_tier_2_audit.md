# SecondLook Tier 2 Engineering & Scientific Audit — Missense→Small-Molecule Binding Plausibility Signal

## Summary
- The Tier 2 claim is defensible ONLY as a narrow, explicitly-hedged computational plausibility signal ("this substitution is/isn't near, and may perturb, the modeled small-molecule binding site"), and must NOT be presented as a resistance/sensitivity prediction — because the pipeline's core assumption (rigid-receptor docking on a wild-type structure detects binding change) fails on the majority of your own nine validation cases, several of which cause resistance through *conformational-equilibrium shifts*, not local steric change at the drug contact.
- Two traps are V1 blockers that will produce scientifically wrong output if shipped as-is: (1) covalent inhibitors (osimertinib, and the C797S case) scored by non-covalent Vina/mCSM-lig, and (2) direct UniProt→PDB residue numbering without SIFTS mapping. Both must be gated/fixed before any demo.
- The best V1 outcome is the "fallback" your plan already contemplates: lead with AlphaMissense + binding-pocket-proximity context, and treat the Vina/mCSM-lig delta as a secondary, clearly-labeled, low-confidence signal — because no published benchmark shows AutoDock Vina score-deltas reliably predict point-mutation ΔΔG, and mCSM-lig's own ceiling is ρ≈0.67 only on crystal complexes with the drug physically bound.

## Key Findings

**1. The central scientific risk: your validation set is dominated by mechanisms rigid WT docking cannot see.** (Analogy: rigid-receptor docking on a WT structure is like checking whether a key fits a *photograph* of a lock taken before the lock was changed — it can catch a key that's now physically too fat for the keyhole, but it cannot see that the lock's internal tumblers have re-sprung into a different resting position.) Of the nine gold-standard cases, only a minority are "clean" local-steric or local-H-bond changes at the drug contact that a WT-structure delta method could plausibly detect:
- **ABL1 T315I + imatinib** — direct steric clash from the bulkier isoleucine plus loss of the T315 side-chain-hydroxyl hydrogen bond to imatinib. Detectable in principle by a delta method.
- **EGFR C797S + osimertinib** — loss of the covalent cysteine anchor. NOT detectable by non-covalent scoring (see Trap 1).
- **EGFR T790M + gefitinib/erlotinib** — resistance is driven substantially by *increased ATP affinity* (T790M raises the mutant's affinity for ATP so ATP outcompetes the reversible inhibitor), not simply by steric block; a binding-affinity delta for the inhibitor alone may not capture it.
- **KIT D816V, KIT V560G, BRAF V600E, ALK G1202R, ALK I1171T** — these act largely by *shifting the kinase conformational equilibrium* (activation-loop / DFG-in vs DFG-out, active vs inactive). Imatinib and vemurafenib bind specific conformations; the mutation changes which conformation is populated. D816V destabilizes the inactive conformation imatinib requires; V600E stabilizes the active DFG-in conformation vemurafenib binds; G1202R is a solvent-front substitution that sterically blocks the inhibitor. A single in-place mutation on one WT structure followed by docking structurally cannot represent an equilibrium shift. BRAF V600E is a positive control precisely because vemurafenib binds the active (DFG-in) state that V600E stabilizes — but a WT apo/holo template will not encode that population change.

Implication: **even a perfectly-engineered pipeline may fail the 70% directionality bar**, and it may fail specifically on the conformational cases while passing the steric ones. This is a boundary of the method, not a bug. Record raw outputs first (as planned) and be prepared to invoke the fallback.

**2. Covalent inhibitor trap is a correctness blocker.** Osimertinib is an irreversible covalent inhibitor that forms a bond to EGFR Cys797. mCSM-lig and AutoDock Vina are non-covalent scoring methods, and as Xu et al. (CarsiDock-Cov, PMC12647997, 2025) state verbatim, "When it comes to covalent inhibitors, conventional docking algorithms usually fail to simulate the covalent binding between the ligand and protein" — the bond-formation energy is simply not in the scoring function, so scores come out low and meaningless. For **EGFR C797S + osimertinib**, the entire resistance mechanism is loss of the covalent anchor: a non-covalent score cannot represent the mechanism, and may even report *retained* binding (the reversible pre-covalent pose still fits the pocket), yielding a confidently wrong "retained binding" answer for a textbook resistance case. Covalent drugs are a real minority worth gating (~7% of FDA-approved drugs, ~128 molecules per Dalton et al.). **Fix: a mechanism gate must detect covalent warheads / known covalent drugs and the target cysteine, and return a `mechanism_invalidated` StructuralSignal instead of a docking score.**

**3. Residue-numbering trap (SIFTS) is a correctness blocker.** UniProt canonical sequence numbering and PDB author residue numbering frequently disagree (cleaved signal peptides, construct/expression-tag numbering, missing loops, isoforms, engineered residues). Mutating "residue 175" in a PDB chain by trusting UniProt numbering can silently mutate the *wrong residue*. The PDBe **SIFTS** resource (Structure Integration with Function, Taxonomy and Sequences; run by PDBe and UniProt at EMBL-EBI) exists specifically to provide up-to-date, weekly-updated residue-level UniProt↔PDB mapping and is the standard dependency for exactly this. Your Step 1 already hard-gates on reference-residue mismatch against the *UniProt* sequence — but that protects the AlphaMissense/UniProt path, NOT the PDB-coordinate path where the mutation and docking actually happen. **Fix: use SIFTS (or an explicit residue-identity check at the PDB coordinate level) before mutating/scoring any PDB chain, and hard-fail if the mapped residue's identity ≠ expected wild-type.**

**4. AlphaMissense is a stability/structural-context proxy, not an oncogenicity or actionability oracle — your framing is correct.** AlphaMissense predicts *pathogenicity* (probability a variant is disease-causing). Per Cheng et al., Science 2023 (adg7492), as implemented in the Ensembl VEP AlphaMissense plugin: a variant is "'likely_benign' if 'am_pathogenicity' < 0.34; 'likely_pathogenic' if 'am_pathogenicity' > 0.564; 'ambiguous' otherwise. These thresholds were chosen to achieve 90% precision for both pathogenic and benign ClinVar variants." But pathogenicity ≠ oncogenicity ≠ actionability: cancer *driver* status requires a selective growth advantage, and multiple analyses note these predictors "are generally trained to predict germline pathogenic variants, and their utility in identifying somatic mutations that drive cancer remains uncharacterized." AlphaMissense also does not predict the *direction* of any drug-binding change. Use it exactly as you propose: a structural/functional-disruption prior, never the clinical relevance signal. (Analogy: AlphaMissense tells you the component is probably out of spec; it does not tell you whether *this* out-of-spec part is the one steering the car, nor which replacement part to order.)

**5. mCSM-lig: know its training target and its hard input requirement.** Per Pires, Blundell & Ascher (Sci Rep 2016, 6:29575), mCSM-lig was trained on the Platinum database to predict the affinity fold-change of a single point mutation and "achieved a Pearson correlation coefficient of ρ = 0.627 over the entire data set and ρ = 0.674 for those mutations close to the ligand" (545 mutations ≤5 Å; the abstract's headline "up to ρ = 0.67" refers to this near-ligand regime — its best case, i.e. drug-contacting residues). Crucially, **it requires a protein–ligand complex with the ligand (HET) physically present in the structure**, because its graph signature encodes protein-to-ligand atom distances. On independent (non-training) test sets its correlation drops materially (to R≈0.43 on a TKI set and ~0.25 on another), indicating limited generalizability. Treat mCSM-lig output as unavailable/low-confidence whenever (a) there is no bona-fide bound ligand matching the candidate drug, (b) the mutation is distal (>5 Å) from the ligand, or (c) the "complex" is a modeled/docked pose rather than a crystal complex.

**6. AutoDock Vina delta for point mutations: no accuracy figure exists for this exact task.** Vina scores are approximate, stochastic (seeded iterated local search; independent runs give different top scores; the documentation recommends exhaustiveness ≥32 for consistency), and — per the Vina documentation and community guidance — are "not experimental binding free energies… useful for rough ranking within a consistent protocol." Vina and AutoDock4 scores are on different scales and must never be mixed. There is **no published Pearson/Spearman for "Vina-delta" (mutant minus WT score) vs experimental ΔΔG on point mutations**; the one direct evaluation (Pan, Portelli, Nguyen & Ascher, Briefings in Bioinformatics 2026, 27(1):bbag035) reports that scoring functions including Vina *failed* to identify drug-resistant mutations, on which dedicated mutation-based methods (mCSM-lig, PremPLI, Rosetta flexddg) succeeded. This is the single most important epistemic fact in this audit: **your primary-traffic method (Vina, used because most candidates lack a PDB HET) has no validated accuracy for the question you are asking it.**

**7. Structure choice drives everything, and predicted structures degrade results measurably.** Docking-readiness ranking: holo (ligand-bound) crystal > apo crystal > homologous holo template > AlphaFold apo > de novo fold. Apo structures dock worse than holo; AlphaFold models are "not suitable for high-throughput docking without post-modeling refinement," and even with excellent backbone accuracy and high pLDDT, side-chain rotamer errors in the pocket break docking. Pan et al. (Briefings in Bioinformatics 2026) quantify the mutation-effect-prediction degradation versus a crystal baseline: "a performance drop ranging from 5% to 30% across different computational models. Specifically, using experimental receptors with docked ligands resulted in a ~5% drop, similar to that observed with AlphaFold3 models (~5%)… AlphaFold2 receptors with docking led to a greater performance loss (10%–20%)… low-identity homology models showed over 30% decline," with the docked-ligand pose (RMSD ~5 Å on predicted receptors) as the dominant error source. Implication: **when you fall back to AlphaFold (Step 3), down-weight or block the binding-delta and lean on pocket-proximity + pLDDT context, not on a docking number.**

**8. Physics-based methods are the accurate-but-expensive alternative — a reality check, not a V1 dependency.** For kinase resistance specifically, alchemical free-energy (FEP/pmx) and Rosetta REF15 are the methods that actually work. Aldeghi, Gapsys & de Groot (ACS Cent Sci 2019, 5:1468): "combining A99 and R15, we obtained a particularly strong consensus estimate… with RMSE = 0.62 kcal/mol, r = 0.71, and AUPRC = 0.61" on 144 experimental Abl ΔΔG values. Hauser et al. (Commun Biol 2018): "With 88% accuracy, resistance or sensitivity to therapy is computed for 144 clinically-identified point mutations in this major target in chronic myelogenous leukemia." These are far beyond a V1 hackathon scope (force-field setup, sampling, compute), but they define the ceiling and confirm that docking-delta is the wrong tool for a quantitative claim. Do not build FEP for V1; cite it as *why* the Tier 2 claim stays qualitative.

## Details

### Pipeline-by-pipeline audit

**Step 1 — Mutation parsing / UniProt fetch / reference gate.** Sound: normalizing R175H / HGVS, hard-gating on reference-residue mismatch against UniProt, rejecting non-missense. Silent-failure risks: UniProt REST returns an isoform whose numbering differs from the clinical report; network/None fetch; ambiguous gene→UniProt accession. Changes: pin the UniProt isoform explicitly in provenance; on fetch failure emit a typed error (`sequence_unavailable`), never proceed. This gate does NOT cover the PDB coordinate path — see Step 3.

**Step 2 — AlphaMissense via Ensembl VEP.** Sound: using paper thresholds not Ensembl's label; treating AM as structural/functional context. Risks: VEP returns no AM score (not all transcripts/variants covered) → must be a typed `alphamissense_unavailable`, not a silent skip; transcript/isoform mismatch between VEP and your UniProt sequence. Change: record AM score, class, the transcript, and model version in provenance; report ambiguous (0.34–0.564) as ambiguous, never rounded to a side.

**Step 3 — Structure sourcing.** Sound: RCSB-first preferring ligand-bound; AlphaFold fallback with residue pLDDT from CA B-factors; not refolding the mutant. Risks: (a) selecting a holo structure whose bound ligand is unrelated to the candidate drug and treating it as usable context; (b) chain selection in multi-chain crystals; (c) **no SIFTS mapping** before using PDB coordinates; (d) an AlphaFold model used for docking as if crystal-quality. Changes: require SIFTS (or explicit residue-identity verification) at coordinate level; record structure source, PDB/AF ID, chain, resolution, and per-residue pLDDT; set a policy that AlphaFold structures block the binding-delta and only yield pocket-proximity + pLDDT context.

**Step 4 — Candidate generation (DGIdb→Open Targets→ChEMBL, PubChem SMILES).** Sound: fallback chain, per-candidate isolation, token-bucket rate limiting. Risks: these sources aggregate interactions of widely varying quality (DGIdb ingests crowd-sourced Drug Target Commons and text-mined claims, and includes broad classes like "mtor inhibitors"); candidates may be prodrugs, salts, metabolites, inactive stereoisomers, PAINS/aggregators, or wrong-target-class molecules. Feeding these to a docking pipeline manufactures false precision. Changes: PAINS/BRENK structural filter via RDKit (with the documented caveat that PAINS alerts are noisy and some legitimate drugs trip them — flag, don't silently delete); normalize salts to parent; resolve identity by InChIKey (below); label each candidate's source and clinical-engagement status.

**Step 5 / 5b — Binding scoring (mCSM-lig primary; Vina fallback carrying most traffic).** The scientific heart and the weakest link. mCSM-lig needs a real bound ligand; Vina-delta has no validated accuracy for point mutations; Vina receptor prep (PDBFixer/OpenMM/meeko/RDKit) fails on multi-chain, alternate conformations, protonation/parameterization, and meeko valence. Changes: (a) mechanism gate before scoring (covalent); (b) mandatory local minimization after in-place mutation (below); (c) fixed seed + exhaustiveness ≥32 + a defined grid box centered on the mapped mutated residue / known pocket; (d) redock the native ligand as a positive control where available and refuse to trust the run if the native pose is not recovered; (e) never report a Vina failure as a pLDDT/structure failure — use distinct typed errors.

**Step 6 — Threshold labeling (not finalized).** Correct instinct: choose the cutoff empirically, version it as a code constant, document it as an internal heuristic. But you cannot put mCSM-lig (log fold-change, dedicated ΔΔG regressor) and Vina-delta (approximate kcal/mol from a scoring function, different scale) on one threshold. Use **method-specific cutoffs**, or, better for V1, collapse to a coarse 3-way (likely_reduced / likely_retained_or_increased / uncertain) with a wide "uncertain" band, defaulting to `uncertain` whenever the confidence conditions below are not met.

**Step 7 — Orchestration / graph-ready output.** Sound design: one entry point, per-candidate loop, API-shaped items, single shared disclaimer, and emitting StructuralSignal objects rather than writing FalkorDB directly (correct — this avoids coupling Tier 2 to graph internals; Tier 1 should own persistence). Add post-condition checks: every returned item carries method, structure id/source, AM score/class, pLDDT-at-residue (when applicable), timestamp, pipeline version, live/cached flag, and the disclaimer.

### Validation guidance
- **Record raw first.** Log AM score/class, structure source+id, method, delta, pLDDT, seed, grid, and native-pose-recovery RMSD for all nine cases BEFORE choosing any cutoff. Do not tune after seeing labels.
- **Expect and pre-declare partial failure.** The conformational cases (KIT D816V, ALK G1202R, ALK I1171T — and mechanistically BRAF V600E and EGFR T790M) are the ones a WT-structure delta is least able to call. If you clear 70% it will likely be by getting the steric/H-bond cases right (T315I; possibly V560G's sensitivity via proximity). If the two designated positive controls (BRAF V600E/vemurafenib and EGFR T790M/osimertinib retained) do not *both* show retained/increased binding, invoke the fallback: drop the delta/label from the UI, keep AM + pocket-proximity.
- **Unit handling.** Never merge mCSM-lig and Vina deltas onto one threshold. Report method-tagged, method-specific bands; default to `uncertain` when confidence conditions fail.
- **Covalent cases are excluded from the docking metric.** Osimertinib cases should be handled by the mechanism gate and scored as `mechanism_invalidated`, not counted as docking hits/misses — otherwise you are grading the wrong tool and polluting your 70% figure.

### Architecture recommendations
- **Mechanism gate (V1, first in scoring):** maintain a small curated covalent-drug list (osimertinib, afatinib, dacomitinib, neratinib, ibrutinib, etc.) plus acrylamide/warhead SMARTS; check whether the target nucleophile (e.g., EGFR C797) is present in the mapped structure. If covalent → `mechanism_invalidated` with a plain-language reason.
- **SIFTS mapping (V1):** map UniProt residue → PDB (chain, author number) via PDBe SIFTS; verify mapped residue identity == expected WT; emit a typed hard-fail otherwise.
- **Minimization (V1):** after in-place mutation, run a short local energy minimization (OpenMM/L-BFGS) with backbone and ligand heavy atoms restrained, relaxing only the mutated side chain and immediate neighbors; a few hundred steps to relieve clashes is reasonable for V1 (the goal is clash relief, not conformational sampling). Do NOT run unrestrained minimization — it drifts the pose and invalidates the WT/mutant comparison.
- **Ligand identity (V1):** resolve PubChem SMILES → RDKit → InChIKey; match against RCSB chemcomp InChIKey. Try exact (full 27-char InChIKey) first; fall back to the connectivity layer (first 14 chars) with an explicit "stereo/charge-insensitive match" flag. Never match drug name → HET code by string.
- **PAINS/aggregator filter (V1-light):** RDKit PAINS/BRENK; flag and down-weight rather than silently drop (PAINS alerts are noisy and some approved drugs trip them); normalize salts to parent; note prodrug/metabolite where known.
- **Graph-ready output shape:** `StructuralSignal { signal_type, variant, gene, drug{name, inchikey, source}, method, delta_score, method_units, label, confidence, alphamissense{score, class, model_version}, structure{source, id, chain, resolution, plddt_at_residue}, mechanism_flag, live_or_cached, generated_at, pipeline_version, disclaimer }`. Distinct node type from the Tier 1 EvidenceItem; the UI must render it in a visually distinct, clearly "computed hypothesis" style.
- **Caching/provenance:** cache per (variant, drug-InChIKey, structure-id, pipeline_version); store all provenance fields + source-DB versions + a regeneration script; always surface the live/cached flag; never present cached as live.
- **Disclaimer (travels with every result):** "Tier 2 is a computational plausibility signal for research and hypothesis generation only. It is not clinical evidence, a diagnosis, or a treatment recommendation."

### Implementation checklist
**Lead-owned (correctness-critical):** mechanism gate; SIFTS mapping + identity hard-fail; minimization protocol + restraints; threshold policy (method-specific, wide uncertain band); StructuralSignal schema + post-condition checks; validation harness (raw-first logging); typed error taxonomy (no swallowed exceptions).
**Junior-delegable (well-scoped):** InChIKey resolution + RCSB chemcomp match; PAINS/BRENK + salt normalization; PubChem token-bucket robustness; caching layer + regen script + version stamps; unit tests per external source (success, empty, timeout, malformed); disclaimer plumbing.
**Integration checks:** native-ligand redock control; end-to-end on all nine cases; covalent cases route to `mechanism_invalidated`; AlphaFold-sourced cases route to pocket+pLDDT-only; every result carries full provenance + disclaimer + live/cached flag.

### Alternatives and corrections
- **Correction:** Do not present Vina-delta as the primary answer. It carries most traffic but has the least validated accuracy for this task. Either restrict Tier 2's quantitative claim to cases with a real bound ligand + contact-residue mutation (mCSM-lig's sweet spot, ρ≈0.67), or demote the delta to a secondary signal and lead with AM + pocket-proximity. Required before demo.
- **Simplification (recommended for V1):** ship the "fallback" as the *default* — AlphaMissense class + binding-pocket proximity (distance of the mutated residue to any bound ligand / known pocket) + pLDDT, with the docking delta shown only when confidence conditions are met and clearly labeled secondary. Lower cost, far fewer ways to be wrong, and honest.
- **Not worth building for V1:** FEP/alchemical methods (accurate but far out of scope); mutant refolding; ESM Atlas on the live path.
- **Trade-off summary:** the honest narrow claim ("the mutation is/isn't in the modeled binding pocket, and a non-covalent estimate suggests a possible/negligible change, low confidence") is less impressive than "predicts resistance," but it is the only claim the evidence supports. Per the project's "no larp" principle, ship the narrow claim.

## Recommendations
1. **Before any demo (blockers):** implement the covalent mechanism gate; SIFTS residue mapping with identity hard-fail; InChIKey ligand identity matching; typed errors everywhere (no silent None/empty list/swallowed exceptions). Threshold: if any of these is missing, do not show a binding label at all.
2. **Before trusting the label:** run the nine-case validation raw-first; require *both* positive controls to pass; if not, switch the UI to AM + pocket-proximity only. Threshold to keep the delta: ≥70% directionality AND both positive controls showing retained/increased binding.
3. **Default posture:** lead with AlphaMissense + pocket-proximity + pLDDT; treat the Vina/mCSM-lig delta as secondary, method-tagged, low-confidence, with a wide "uncertain" band. Default to `uncertain` when: covalent drug, no matching bound ligand, mutation >5 Å from the ligand, AlphaFold/apo source, or a failed native-pose redock control.
4. **Provenance + disclaimer on every result; StructuralSignal must be a distinct type from EvidenceItem; cached ≠ live must always be visible.**
5. **What would change these recommendations:** a bound holo crystal for the specific gene+drug (upgrades mCSM-lig into its ρ≈0.67 regime and would justify a stronger, still-hedged claim), or adoption of a validated covalent-aware / conformational-ensemble method. Absent those, keep the claim narrow.

## Caveats
- The nine-case set is too small to *set* a robust threshold; use it to *reject* a bad one, not to calibrate a precise cutoff.
- mCSM-lig's (and PremPLI's) high headline correlations are partly inflated by training-set overlap with Platinum; independent-set performance is materially lower (R≈0.43 on a TKI set).
- PAINS filters are themselves noisy (some approved drugs trip alerts); use them as a flag, not an auto-delete.
- The mechanistic annotations for the validation cases are drawn from the primary literature and are robust, but the *directional prediction* your pipeline will produce for each is exactly what the raw-first validation run must establish empirically — do not assume the pipeline will reproduce the known biology.
- All Tier 2 outputs are computational plausibility only — never clinical evidence, a diagnosis, or a treatment recommendation.
